# Karachi boutique website pilot

Draft sales sheet for the first local shop conversations. Review the scope and price before quoting a client.

## The offer

**A branded online shop for a Karachi clothing boutique**

**Suggested first-client price: PKR 55,000** for a tightly scoped launch. Treat this as a starting quote, not a fixed market rate. Confirm the shop's needs and costs before accepting the work.

### Included in the pilot scope

- Apply the shop's name, logo, colours, contact details and social links to the storefront.
- Set up up to 3 product categories and upload up to 25 products using photos and details supplied by the shop.
- Configure size and colour choices where the shop's products need them.
- Set up the product catalogue, cart, order review and admin screens for products and orders.
- Agree and configure one real order path before launch. The current demo checkout is presentation-only and does not process real orders.
- Make two reasonable revision rounds and provide one owner handover session.
- Include 30 days for fixing launch defects within the agreed scope.

### Quoted separately

- Domain registration and hosting.
- Payment gateway approval or integration, courier booking/tracking, SMS/WhatsApp provider fees, paid software, product photography, copywriting, and ongoing maintenance.
- More than 25 products, extra design rounds, or custom features.

Do not promise payment, courier, tax, marketing, search-ranking, or security outcomes until the specific work has been scoped and verified. Give the client a written list of recurring costs and account owners before launch.

I’ve made a boutique demo to show the idea: [public demo link]. If you’re considering selling online, could I visit or call for 10 minutes to learn how you currently take orders? I can then show you a short plan and clear price. No pressure.

Use this only after replacing `[Name]` and `[public demo link]`. The current `http://127.0.0.1:8000` address is local to your computer and cannot be shared with a shop.

## First meeting questions

1. Where do most orders come from today: walk-ins, Instagram, WhatsApp, or marketplaces?
2. Which 20–25 products should be online first, and who can supply the photos, prices, sizes and stock?
3. How should customers place an order: WhatsApp confirmation, cash on delivery, bank transfer, or online payment?
4. Which courier do you use, and who handles delivery charges, returns and failed deliveries?
5. Who will update products and confirm new orders each day?
6. What budget and launch date should the proposal fit?

## Simple next steps with a shop

1. Ask permission for a 10-minute conversation; do not send repeated follow-ups if they decline or do not reply.
2. Learn the shop's order and delivery process before showing features.
3. Show the public demo, then agree on one small product collection and one real order path.
4. Send a one-page written quote with inclusions, exclusions, milestone dates, recurring costs and who owns the domain/hosting accounts.
5. Start only after the shop approves the written scope and provides the product assets.
